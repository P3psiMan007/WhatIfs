What if an extreme solar storm hit Earth tomorrow? A coronal mass ejection leaves the Sun and crosses ninety three million miles in under a day. It slams into the magnetosphere and squeezes it. Satellites in low orbit feel the atmosphere swell beneath them and start to drag. GPS timing drifts because the ionosphere is no longer a predictable layer. High frequency radio goes quiet across the daylight side of the planet. On the ground, the changing field pushes direct current into long transmission lines. Transformers were never designed to carry it and they begin to heat. The grid protects itself by tripping, and the lights go out across whole regions. Recovery is slow because the largest transformers are built to order.
