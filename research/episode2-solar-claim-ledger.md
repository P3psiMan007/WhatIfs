Date checked: 2026-08-10
Working premise: What if an extreme solar storm hit the modern world?
Rule: every material scientific/historical claim in narration must map to this ledger; uncertainty stays explicit.

## Claims intentionally excluded or constrained

- **`18 hours warning` as a hard promise** — not safe as a universal claim. A fast CME can arrive in ~15 hours, but warning is not simply a fixed countdown. Package A must avoid an exact countdown unless the script defines an explicit hypothetical CME speed.
- **`The internet goes down worldwide`** — excluded. Space weather can directly affect satellites, radio/navigation and power infrastructure, while internet outages would largely be secondary/local dependency effects.
- **`Every phone/car/computer gets fried like an EMP`** — excluded. The strongest direct induction risk is tied to large-scale geoelectric fields coupling into long grounded conductors; local consumer devices are not automatically destroyed.
- **`Years without power` / multi-trillion-dollar loss** — excluded from narration unless tied to a clearly labeled scenario study. Old extreme-event estimates are too often repeated as certainty.
- **Exact recurrence probability for Carrington-class storms** — excluded. Estimates are model-sensitive and unnecessary for this story.
- **`No limit to solar storms`** — prohibited. The 2026 paper challenges an assumed upper limit in Earth's current response; it does not prove literally unlimited physical effects.
