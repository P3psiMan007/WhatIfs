### 1) What if AI's electricity demand grows faster than the grid can handle? — 9.18/10

**Why now**
- Semrush: `AI data centers` ~12.1K US monthly searches with a strongly rising recent trend.
- Gartner (2026-06-10) projects worldwide data-center electricity consumption at 565 TWh in 2026, up 26% YoY; AI-optimized servers account for 31% of data-center electricity use, with AI-server consumption projected to rise 84.2% YoY in 2026.
- IEA (2026-04-16) says data-centre electricity use surged in 2025; five large tech companies spent more than $400B in 2025 and their capex was set to rise another 75% in 2026.
- EIA (2026-03-12) says data centers are driving renewed US electricity-demand growth; EIA's 2026 outlook calls data-center load an emerging dominant driver of long-term US electricity growth.
- On 2026-08-03 Reuters reported record US power demand continuing to rise partly because of AI/data-center infrastructure. Current political/local backlash is also generating fresh discussion.

**Fresh angle / content gap**
Do not make a generic `AI wastes electricity` video. Build a systems thought experiment: *what if compute demand grows faster than generation, transmission, and interconnection can be built?* Follow the competition for scarce grid capacity from server rack -> substation -> city -> national buildout, then reveal the non-doom outcomes: new generation, batteries, flexible workloads, grid expansion, pricing rules, and location shifts.

**Why it fits the channel**
A concrete counterfactual with visible consequences, diagrams, maps, escalating scale, and a nuanced snap-back. It expands the channel from biology into technology/infrastructure without abandoning the `big question -> consequence chain -> surprising resolution` promise.

**Risk**
Easy to become a dry energy-policy explainer. Script must stay human: what happens to your bill, your city, a hospital, a factory, and the server farm when power is scarce? Avoid claiming blackouts are inevitable.

**Primary package hypotheses**
1. **What If AI Uses More Power Than the Grid Can Handle?** — thumbnail: glowing data center on one side, dim city on the other, one overloaded cable between them; text `NOT ENOUGH POWER`.
2. **What Happens If AI Runs Out of Electricity?** — thumbnail: server rack at 100%, grid gauge in red; text `POWER LIMIT`.
3. **AI Is Building a New Power Problem** — thumbnail: giant server rack plugged into a city-scale substation; text `WHO GETS THE POWER?`.

**First-15-second hook concept**
`Imagine the AI boom does not run out of chips. It runs out of electricity. Not in some distant future — a new data center asks your region for as much power as a city, and the grid has to decide what gets built, who pays, and what waits.` Then immediately clarify that this is a stress-test scenario, not a prediction of inevitable blackouts.

**Visual ladder**
GPU/server -> rack -> data hall -> substation -> local grid -> city loads competing -> regional map -> generation buildout -> flexible-compute solution -> final balanced grid.

### 2) What if the next extreme solar storm is stronger than we planned for? — 9.10/10

**Why now**
- NASA (2026-07-15) reported a NASA-led Nature study suggesting the assumed ceiling on Earth's response to increasingly strong solar wind may be an illusion.
- NOAA documents direct consequences for power grids, GPS/GNSS, satellites, aviation and communications.
- Search demand is large: solar flare ~40.5K, solar storm ~27.1K, Carrington event ~22.2K US monthly searches.

**Fresh angle / content gap**
Avoid the old generic `Carrington Event destroys the internet` trope. The fresh premise is the new NASA result: if Earth's upper-atmosphere currents do not saturate the way scientists thought, what changes in an extreme but physically plausible event — and what *doesn't* automatically collapse?

**Strengths**
Highest visual potential in the pool: Sun, CME, aurora, satellites, GPS error, transformers, aviation, warning timeline. Excellent channel coherence and evergreen shelf life.

**Risk**
Historically saturated topic. The title/thumbnail must communicate the new 2026 scientific angle, not look like another apocalypse slideshow.

**Package hypotheses**
1. **What If the Next Solar Storm Is Stronger Than We Planned For?** — `NO SAFETY CEILING?`
2. **NASA Just Changed the Worst-Case Solar Storm** — `EARTH'S LIMIT?`
3. **What Would an Extreme Solar Storm Actually Break?** — `GPS • GRID • SATELLITES`

### 3) What if AI removes the first rung of the career ladder? — 9.03/10

**Why now**
- PwC 2026 AI Jobs Barometer analysed more than one billion job ads across 27 countries/territories.
- In 2.4M US entry-level postings, the most AI-exposed junior roles are now 7x more likely to demand traditionally senior human skills; `seniorised` entry roles grew 35% since 2019 while other entry roles declined 10%.
- PwC explicitly describes AI removing some routine work that once functioned as apprenticeship.
- Broader `AI jobs` search demand is ~14.8K US monthly searches; the exact `AI entry level jobs` phrase is much smaller (~590), so demand is broad-topic rather than exact-query driven.

**Fresh angle**
Not `AI takes jobs`. The stronger question is: *if AI automates the beginner tasks that taught people how to become experts, where do future experts come from?*

**Strengths**
High emotional/relatable stakes, strong ladder visual metaphor, excellent retention structure, current debate.

**Risk**
Evidence is contested and causality is hard. The script must present counter-evidence and avoid claiming AI is already the sole cause of weak graduate hiring.

### 4) What if everyone worked four days a week? — 8.48/10

Solid broad-interest evergreen with real-world evidence and relatable visuals, but weaker current momentum and less mystery than the top three.

## Decision

**Winner: AI electricity / grid-capacity stress test.**

It narrowly beats the solar-storm candidate because it combines a currently rising demand signal, unusually fresh 2026 news momentum, strong primary-source depth, a visible real-world content gap for a concise high-quality systems explainer, and enough visual/narrative escalation for 8–10 minutes. The solar-storm topic remains the highest-priority evergreen backup if the AI-grid script/package fails its pre-production kill gate.

## Kill gate for the winner

Do **not** proceed to final script unless all are true:
- The script can explain grid capacity in plain English without turning into policy jargon.
- It has a human-scale opening and at least three escalating consequence layers.
- It distinguishes electricity *energy* (TWh) from peak *power* (GW/MW).
- It does not imply AI is the only source of load growth or that blackouts are inevitable.
- It includes at least one credible adaptation path (new generation/grid, batteries, flexible compute, siting/pricing) so the payoff is not doom-only.
- At least one title/thumbnail pair remains compelling after factual wording is tightened.
