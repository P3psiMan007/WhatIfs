## Default package

**Title:** Your Brain Can Sleep While You're Still Awake

**Thumbnail visual:** Close-up illustrated human face split vertically. Left eye open and alert; right eye closed. Inside the head, one side of a simplified brain is bright/active while the other side has slow-wave arcs and a dim sleeping treatment. Very dark background, warm off-white face/line art, one amber accent, one muted blue sleep accent. No clutter, no extra objects.

**Thumbnail text:** HALF ASLEEP?

**Curiosity mechanism:** Contradiction. The viewer believes awake/asleep is binary; the package shows both states at once.

**Promise:** The video will explain how parts of a brain can express sleep-like activity during wakefulness and whether humans can ever do the dolphin-style version.

**Likely failure mode:** If the thumbnail feels like generic sleep advice instead of a biological paradox, CTR will suffer. Keep the split-state brain impossible-looking and instantly readable.

## Default selection rationale

Use the default package first. It states the impossible-sounding fact in plain global English, preserves scientific truth because the video qualifies “part of the brain” immediately, and gives the thumbnail a complementary question instead of repeating the title.

## Thumbnail production rules

- 16:9, 1280×720 or larger.
- One dominant face/brain idea only.
- Text maximum two words for default.
- Must remain legible at ~10% size.
- No red circles/arrows, UI chrome, charts, tiny labels, or busy backgrounds.
- Strong separation between subject and background.
- Do not depict medical gore or realistic exposed brain tissue; use clean illustrated/doodle anatomy.
- Title and image must not imply a demonstrated human sleep-replacement technology.
